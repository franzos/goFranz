When I first read about EUID I thought it could be a pretty nice idea; Instead of scattered KYC services that each store some information about us, the government issues a digital password, that anyone can verify quickly and securely; In theory, it could work much like my physical passport:

- It could be used by anyone, to verify my identity
- there's no need for the issuing government to know, who's seen it

The downside is, that often:

1. They are expensive, and take time to replace
2. We rely on many KYC provider to match passport and other details, with user
3. Can be sold and used by others (they can fetch thousands of euro)*

*That get's increasingly difficult, with facial recognition and fingerprints.

EUID, which is based on OID4VP had the opportunity to solve 1 and 2:

In the future, you may not require an ID card or passport depending on where you travel, but can rely solely on the digital identity card on your phone; Obtaining or replacing this, should be fairly easy, as long as you can proof to the responsible government office, that you are who you claim to be; There's no need to print a booklet, or create a physical ID card.

In addition, with the digital signature from your EUID wallet, you should be able to get trough KYC gates, because instead of a passport scan that could come from anywhere, and a blurry picture of your face, providers can now rely on digital proof that bullet-proof - assuming it's you that's holding the phone. So we may still see some combination of face-scan, and e-signature, but that's a step-up in my view.

Not too bad, unless the authorities abuse this new ....

Well, even though OID4VP is an open standard, and in theory, anyone with a EUID wallet should be able to "identity themselves" to anyone, this is not the case; In fact, to "accept" EUID wallets as authentication / identification method, you need to be registered with the local government. Yes - we have this beautiful open standard, yet the EU decided to box it in - because it would be terrible, if you willingly share your name, with someone that's not approved by the government.

This requitement will likely be enforced by the wallet itself, so in theory you could just use a wallet that doesn't do that, but that probably won't get approved by the government, and thus, probably won't get a EUID issued to it; Let's see who has the last say here - but so far, this is an awesome standard, severly limited by goverments.

Similiar initiatives may be coming to the UK, Australia, and many other parts of the world.
